Final exam: marking guide

For the instructor. Do not hand this out.

Section A: answers 30 marks

One mark per question. Where a question has two or three parts, split the mark between them and round the section total to the nearest whole mark. Accept any wording that shows the student understands the point. The notes say what to accept and what not to.

Any two of: screen readers and other assistive tools use them to describe and jump around the page; search engines understand the page better; other developers, and you later, can read the structure without decoding a wall of divs; they give clear hooks for CSS. Section B: marking the practical 70 marks

Mark each line in the tables on the exam paper. Give part marks freely; a feature that mostly works should get most of its marks. These quick tests cover nearly everything. Open the page with the console showing.

Testing the booking panel

  1. Press plus on Regular twice and on Student three times. With the starting prices the page should show 10500 before the discount, 9450 after it, and "Group discount applied".
  2. Press minus on Student until it stops. It must stop at 0, and the discount must disappear once the order drops under 5 tickets.
  3. Add tickets until the order has 10, then press plus again. The count stays at 10 and the limit message appears. Check this across different ticket types, not just one: 4 Regular, 4 VIP and 2 Student, then one more of any type.
  4. Press Clear, then Book. Expect "Pick at least one ticket."
  5. Add one ticket, type a one-letter name and Book. Expect "Enter your name." Then type a proper name with a 10-digit phone number. Expect "Enter an 11-digit phone number." Then fix the number. Expect the success message with the right count and total, and check that the red error style is gone.
  6. Change one price in the array in script.js and reload. If the total does not change, the total is not coming from the array, so the data line gets 0 and the function line loses at least half.

Testing the rest

  • Click every nav link.
  • Use the device toolbar at 360px and 600px, or check on a phone. Scroll sideways at 360px; if the page moves, the responsive line loses at least 2.
  • Press Tab through the page. Links and buttons need a visible focus style.
  • Search the HTML for style=". Any match costs the inline-styles mark.
  • Read the total function. It must loop over the array and return a number. A function that only sets text on the page and returns nothing gets at most 3 of the 6.
  • Check that the Book handler reads .value inside the click function. Reading it once at the top of the file is the bug from question 30 and costs 2 marks.

notes.txt

Two marks per answer. Give 2 when the answer is specific to the student's own code, 1 when it is correct but general enough to have been written without seeing the project, and 0 when it is missing or wrong. For question 2 the student should mention the loop, adding each price times its count, and returning the result. For question 3 they should describe each property in the technique they used, as it behaves on their page.

When you ask a student to explain

Pick two or three lines from their script.js, usually the total function and the Book handler, and ask what each one does and what would happen if it were removed. A student who wrote the code can answer quickly even if they are nervous. If they cannot explain a part at all, take away the marks for that part.
